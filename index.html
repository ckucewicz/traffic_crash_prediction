01 — The Problem

A City Working Toward Zero Traffic Deaths

In 2023, Chicago recorded 136 traffic deaths. In response, the city launched its Vision Zero Action Plan in 2017, committing to eliminate traffic fatalities and serious injuries by 2026.

Traffic safety is also an equity issue — Black and Brown communities on Chicago's South and West Sides bear a disproportionate share of crash hospitalizations.

02 — Spatial Analysis

Where Do Crashes Concentrate?

Crash density clusters heavily along Chicago's major north-south arterials — Western, Pulaski, and Cicero Avenues — and throughout the downtown Loop corridor.

These hotspots align with high traffic volume, but volume alone doesn't explain injury severity. The next maps dig deeper.

Serious Crashes Are Spread Differently Than Volume

Serious crashes are not simply a function of where crashes are most frequent. They spread into residential areas and corridors with fewer overall incidents, suggesting road design and speed environment play a larger role than raw volume.

04 — Neighborhood Equity

Serious Injury Rates Vary Sharply by Neighborhood

Aggregating to Chicago's 77 community areas reveals a pattern that crash counts alone obscure: some lower-volume neighborhoods have significantly higher serious injury rates.

Communities on the South and West Sides — which face documented underinvestment in traffic infrastructure — show elevated rates, reinforcing the equity framing of Chicago's Vision Zero plan.

05 — Model Insights

What Predicts a Serious Crash?

A decision tree model (PR AUC = 0.096) was selected for its interpretability — essential for policy audiences who need to understand and trust model outputs.

Airbag non-deployment is the top predictor, likely acting as a proxy for vehicle speed and size at impact. Male sex and seasonal extremes also contribute meaningfully.

Note: feature importances reflect entropy reduction, not effect direction or magnitude.

Policy Recommendations

The model and spatial analysis together support three actionable directions for CDOT, CMAP, and Cook County transportation agencies.

Full Notebook Spatial Notebook Presentation

Recommendations

  • Require annual airbag inspections — particularly for vehicles registered in high-rate community areas
  • Introduce tiered weight fees for heavy personal vehicles (trucks, SUVs) to reduce impact forces in urban crashes
  • Target male driver safety campaigns toward aggressive/reckless driving behaviors flagged by the model
  • Prioritize Vision Zero infrastructure investment in neighborhoods with high serious-injury rates, not just high crash counts
  • Integrate spatial features (proximity to signals, speed cameras) directly into future model iterations
